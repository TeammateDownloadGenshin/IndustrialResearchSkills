# Biomedical and Healthcare Research

Use this overlay for biotech, biopharma, vaccines, diagnostics, medtech, life-science tools, CRO/CDMO, and healthcare services.

## Evidence layers

1. Regulatory documents and labels: FDA, EMA, NMPA/CDE and other applicable regulators.
2. Trial registries and protocols: ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO ICTRP, EU CTIS, ChiCTR, and jurisdiction-specific registries.
3. Peer-reviewed primary studies and systematic reviews; check corrections, retractions, and data cutoffs.
4. Company filings and official presentations for pipeline ownership, economics, timelines, and disclosed results.
5. Patents and official transaction documents for IP and licensing terms.

Conference abstracts, posters, press releases, KOL comments, media, and broker research may be useful leads but require clear labeling and stronger corroboration.

## Pipeline table

For every material program record: asset, modality, target/mechanism, sponsor/partner, rights geography, indication, biomarker, phase, trial identifier, design, comparator, population, enrollment, primary endpoint, data cutoff, status, next milestone, regulatory designations, and source.

Do not collapse distinct cohorts or indications into a single stage. A program is not validated merely because the target is biologically plausible.

## Clinical assessment

Evaluate randomization, blinding, comparator, multiplicity, estimand, missing data, follow-up, endpoint hierarchy, subgroup prespecification, safety exposure, censoring, and clinical—not only statistical—significance. Distinguish response rate, duration, progression-free survival, overall survival, surrogate endpoints, and patient-reported outcomes.

Compare trials only after reconciling eligibility, prior lines, biomarker prevalence, geography, standard of care, assessment criteria, follow-up, and data maturity. Cross-trial comparisons are hypotheses unless supported by an appropriate comparative design.

## Scientific and technical assessment

Separate target validation, modality feasibility, translational evidence, delivery/manufacturing, dose/exposure, pharmacology, resistance mechanisms, and safety liabilities. Identify whether evidence is human genetic, clinical, animal, in vitro, computational, or management assertion.

## Commercial and reimbursement assessment

Use epidemiology consistent with the treatable population, diagnostic pathway, line of therapy, contraindications, testing rates, payer restrictions, persistence, and capacity constraints. Avoid multiplying headline prevalence by list price.

For medtech and diagnostics, include installed base, utilization, consumables, workflow integration, reimbursement, regulatory class, service requirements, and purchasing decision-makers.

## Biotech valuation

For pre-commercial assets, probability-adjusted NPV should disclose phase-specific probability assumptions, timing, eligible population, penetration, price/net price, launch curve, exclusivity, royalties/milestones, COGS, R&D/SG&A, tax, discount rate, dilution, and terminal treatment. Present sensitivities and never disguise assumed probabilities as empirical truth.

For platform companies, avoid counting the same platform value in both program rNPVs and a separate platform premium without explanation.

## Minimum red-team questions

- Is the target clinically validated in the relevant disease and modality?
- Does the trial endpoint support the intended label and reimbursement claim?
- Is the control arm current standard of care?
- Are response durability and safety exposure mature enough?
- What manufacturing, CMC, diagnostic, enrollment, or financing constraint can delay the catalyst?
- Are partnership economics, opt-ins, milestones, royalties, and geographic rights fully reconciled?
