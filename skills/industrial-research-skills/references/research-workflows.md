# Research Workflows

Select the smallest workflow that covers the decision. Combine modes only when the requested deliverable needs them.

## Industry chain and thematic research

1. Define the product/service boundary, geography, customer, value-chain depth, and time horizon.
2. Map upstream inputs, enabling technologies, production, distribution, end markets, regulators, payers, and substitutes.
3. For each node, record value captured, concentration, capacity, bottlenecks, switching costs, pricing mechanism, and bargaining power.
4. Quantify market size with both top-down and bottom-up checks. Show base year, nominal/real treatment, FX, inclusion rules, and uncertainty range.
5. Identify structural drivers, cyclical drivers, policy dependencies, technology discontinuities, and pass-through mechanisms.
6. Produce a market map, value-chain table, evidence ledger, and a shortlist of falsifiable questions.

## Public-company first pass

Start with filings, investor materials, transcripts, and regulator records. Summarize:

- business model and reporting segments;
- revenue, volume, price/mix, gross margin, operating leverage, cash conversion, and capital intensity;
- customer/supplier concentration and related-party exposure;
- management incentives, ownership, dilution, and capital allocation;
- consensus-sensitive variables, upcoming catalysts, and unresolved accounting or disclosure issues.

Keep a compact three-column ledger: claim, evidence, analyst interpretation.

## Earnings and call analysis

Compare the new period with prior-period actuals, company guidance, and clearly identified expectations. Separate revenue/volume/price/mix effects; recurring and one-off items; reported and adjusted measures; currency and scope changes.

For calls, extract management claims, changed wording, quantitative guidance, questions avoided, contradictions with filings, and commitments that can be checked next period. Do not infer tone scores as facts.

## Competitive analysis and market map

Define the competitive unit before comparing companies. Use like-for-like geography, product, customer, channel, period, and accounting scope. Compare capabilities, switching costs, distribution, regulatory position, intellectual property, manufacturing, data, unit economics, and balance-sheet capacity.

Distinguish direct competitors, substitutes, complements, suppliers, and potential entrants. Record evidence for every material share or ranking claim.

## Unit economics and valuation

Reconstruct unit economics from disclosed inputs where possible. Reconcile customer, order, product, cohort, patient, procedure, site, or installed-base denominators.

Use valuation methods appropriate to economics and maturity: DCF, comparable multiples, sum-of-the-parts, asset value, or probability-adjusted NPV. Show calculation date, share count, net debt/cash, dilution, currency, terminal assumptions, and sensitivity ranges. A valuation is a scenario result, not a fact.

## Catalyst map

For each catalyst, record expected window, source, prerequisite, success condition, failure condition, expected evidence, alternative explanation, and potential thesis impact. Separate scheduled events from inferred timing.

## Thesis red-team

State the strongest thesis in falsifiable terms. Then test:

- which facts would disprove it;
- which assumptions carry most valuation sensitivity;
- what the market may already price in;
- alternative causal explanations;
- accounting, governance, regulatory, technological, financing, and execution failure modes;
- evidence that is missing, stale, circular, or management-supplied.

Conclude with thesis status: supported, mixed, weakened, or unresolved. Avoid forced buy/sell conclusions.

## Report writing

Lead with the answer and decision relevance. A robust structure is: executive view, scope and definitions, industry chain, market and competition, company economics, catalysts, valuation scenarios, risks/counter-evidence, unresolved questions, and sources. Attach a claim–evidence map for every material assertion.
